## Information the service may process

RepairProof is designed to record repair cases. Depending on enabled features, this can include account and organization details; customer contact and authorization records; device make/model and minimized identifiers; reported symptoms; technician findings; quotations and decisions; repair actions and test results; evidence documents and photos; warranty/dispute records; and service/security logs. Do not submit payment-card data, government identity documents, or unrelated sensitive data.

## Purposes and access

Information is used to operate the repair record and authorization workflow, secure accounts, prevent abuse, provide requested support, maintain audit history, and meet applicable recordkeeping duties. Shop personnel receive access according to their assigned role and case assignment. Customer-facing authorization pages expose a deliberately limited case view. Evidence is private and accessed through short-lived, audited links. A content hash can show that stored bytes match a recorded digest; it does not establish who created the evidence or whether it is truthful.

## Choices, rights, and requests

Where required, RepairProof will request and record a valid notice/consent or other lawful basis before processing. Subject to applicable law, a person may request access/export, correction, withdrawal where consent is the basis, deletion, or grievance review by contacting [privacy contact]. The service may need to retain limited records for security, dispute, legal-hold, warranty, or statutory purposes; any refusal or partial fulfilment must state the legal basis and review route.

## Retention and deletion

The published retention schedule must be approved before launch and must specify periods for accounts, repair records, original evidence, derived files, audit events, backups, and legal holds. Deletion requests must remove or de-identify eligible active data and propagate deletion tombstones to derived objects and later backup expiry. No period is promised by this draft.

## Providers and international processing

Before launch, publish the actual providers and purposes used for hosting, database, object storage, email, security monitoring, and AI (if enabled), including processing locations, transfer safeguards, and subprocessors. Do not state that a provider is used or that data stays in a region until its production configuration and contract are verified. AI/document extraction must remain off until its data use, retention, and provider terms are approved.

## Security and incidents

RepairProof uses role checks, tenant scoping, audit history, private object storage, and access controls as implementation controls. No service can promise absolute security. Suspected exposure or loss should be reported immediately to [incident contact]. The controller will assess regulatory and customer notification duties and communicate through verified channels.

Contact [grievance officer/name and monitored address]. Include [response process and appeal/escalation route]. Updates to this notice will show a revision date and, where required, be presented for renewed notice or consent.
